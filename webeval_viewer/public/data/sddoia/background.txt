SDD-OIA is a synthetic driving dataset from the rsbench benchmark. Each picture is a road scene seen from a car. It is labelled with what is in it, such as a red light, a pedestrian or an obstacle ahead, and with the actions that are allowed in it: stop, go forward, turn left and turn right.

The model is a concept bottleneck model, which decides in two steps. First, a vision network looks at the picture and estimates, for each of 19 concepts, the probability that it is present:

    ahead, to go forward   greenLight, follow (a car ahead to follow), clear (the road is clear)
    ahead, to stop         redLight (a red or yellow light), stopSign, car, person, rider, otherObstacle
    on the left            noLeftLane, leftObstacle, leftSolidLine,
                           leftLane (on a left-turn lane), leftFollow (the car ahead turns left)
    on the right           noRightLane, rightObstacle, rightSolidLine,
                           rightLane (on a right-turn lane), rightFollow (the car ahead turns right)

Second, a single layer turns these 19 probabilities into one score per action: outputStop, outputForward, outputLeft and outputRight. The action taken is the one with the highest score, provided that score is above 0. A score above 0 means the model rates the action as more likely allowed than not.

The rules say which action the car must or must not take given what it sees, for example stop at a red light. Every rule shown in this study is violated: the verifier (Marabou) found concept probabilities for which the model does not do what the rule demands.
